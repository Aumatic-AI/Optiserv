ENGAGEMENT PATTERNSaaS, United Kingdom/Dedicated staffing

An engineering pod with a real afternoon overlap

Pattern: 3–5 engineers · 13:30–22:30 IST · 8 h live overlap with London

Shape
Two to four engineers plus a QA engineer, working the client’s backlog, in the client’s repo, inside the client’s stand-up. Employed by us, directed by them.
Why the shift matters
On a standard Indian day a London team gets about three and a half hours of overlap. Moved to 13:30–22:30 IST, the same team is online for eight of London’s nine working hours.
What we carry
Employment, payroll, office seat, equipment, IT and an account manager. The client carries the roadmap and nothing else.

DELIVERED PROJECTAutomation/Built for a marketing agency

Kinetix

One agency ad’s budget now tests many in a day.

The system
Kinetix — an AI ad-generation platform we designed and built, with automated A/B testing and budget reallocation built in.
Problem
Ad production and budget allocation were manual. One concept at a time reached the market, and the spend behind it was reallocated by hand, after the fact.
Effect
Variants are produced, tested and funded without a person in the loop. A single budget exercises many creative variants inside a day instead of one at a time across a cycle.
AI generationA/B testingBudget automation
ENGAGEMENT PATTERNiGaming/QA & testing

A release-QA squad on the operator’s train

Pattern: 4–6 QA engineers · Hyderabad · release-aligned shift

Shape
A lead plus three to five engineers, split between manual release validation and an automated regression suite that grows with each cycle.
Why offshore works here
Release QA is cyclical and intense. Carrying that capacity in-house means paying for peak staffing year-round; carrying it with us means the squad flexes with the release calendar.
Shift
Aligned to the operator’s release windows rather than to Indian office hours, so sign-off never waits a timezone.

DELIVERED PROJECTAutomation/Built for a B2B sales team

Atlas

A 5-tool workflow collapsed into one system.

The system
Atlas — a unified outbound dashboard we designed and built, covering prospecting, personalised outreach and intent classification in one place.
Problem
Outbound ran across five disconnected tools. Prospecting, personalisation and qualification each lived somewhere else, and the handoffs leaked.
Effect
The workflow stops being a chain of exports between tools and becomes a single system with one place to look.
ProspectingIntent classificationConsolidation
ENGAGEMENT PATTERNScale-up/Recruitment & RPO

A hiring surge that does not require hiring recruiters

Pattern: dedicated recruiting team · fixed window · roles land on the client’s payroll

Shape
A recruiting team stood up for a defined push — a funding round, a market entry, a product launch — running sourcing, screening, scheduling and offer management.
Why project rather than full-cycle
The need has an end date. Full-cycle RPO suits a standing hiring function; a surge suits a team that stands down when the surge does.
Where the people land
On the client’s own payroll. This is recruitment, not staffing — we find and vet, the client employs.
Full-cycle sourcingStructured screeningOffer management
ENGAGEMENT PATTERNScaling company/Operational staffing

The back office, staffed before it breaks

Pattern: 2–4 people · people ops, payroll admin and IT helpdesk as one unit

Shape
A small operations unit covering HR records and onboarding, payroll administration and statutory filing, and device provisioning with first-line IT support.
Why it comes up
These tasks rarely get a job title until they are already late. They land on a founder at eleven at night, or on an engineer who was hired to build something else.
What changes
The function gets an owner who is not also the CTO, and the people who should be building go back to building.

ENGAGEMENT PATTERNRegulated sector/QA & testing

An accessibility and performance programme with a deadline

Pattern: scoped project · Section 508 audit · load and penetration testing

Shape
A fixed-scope engagement rather than a standing team: a Section 508 accessibility audit, performance engineering to establish real breaking points, and penetration testing.
Why project rather than headcount
A compliance date is a date. It needs a team that arrives, produces evidence against a named standard, and hands over — not a permanent cost line.
What is handed over
Findings mapped to the standard, each with severity, reproduction steps and the remediation it implies. Evidence an auditor will accept, not a summary.
